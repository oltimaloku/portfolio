## Overview

IDEA, the Interactive Daily Epilepsy Assistant, is the product of [Edee Care](https://www.edeecare.com/), a startup building tools for people living with epilepsy. Seizures rarely come out of nowhere: stress, poor sleep, missed medication, alcohol and flashing lights all play a part, but those patterns are almost impossible to see from memory alone. IDEA makes them visible by pairing a short daily journal with seizure logs and data from the user's own wearables.

I was at Edee Care for almost two years, first as its product manager intern and then as the lead engineer on the app. It's the largest project I've worked on outside Neuron, and the one that taught me how to take a product from an idea to something people actually use.

## Phase I: an SMS pilot before any code

Before building an app, we needed to know the idea held up. As product manager intern I led a small team of two UX designers and a researcher to run an SMS pilot. Through Heymarket, participants received check-in texts twice a day linking to a short Typeform survey. I managed the incoming data, wrote a weekly report on each participant's condition, and interviewed our first users.

The pilot validated the core value proposition and gave us the raw material for the app: which questions people would answer every day, which they skipped, and what they wanted back. From those interviews I designed the user flows, wireframes and requirements documents for six features of the app's MVP.

## Phase II: version one

With the pilot's findings in hand we built the first version. I chose **Flutter** so one codebase would ship to both iOS and Android, with a **Node.js and Express** backend and **MongoDB** through Mongoose. The MVP covered the essentials: logging seizures, recording triggers and tracking medication, with authentication built on bcrypt.

It was basic, but it went out to testers on Apple TestFlight. For most of 2023 I was the only developer on the app, learning Flutter, Node and MongoDB from scratch as I went.

## Phase III: a major overhaul

Feedback from V1 testing, a new visual identity and new designers on the team led to a near-complete rewrite of the front end.

### From parameter passing to MVVM

The original app passed state down through widget parameters. That's fine for a small app but was becoming unmanageable. I moved state into **Provider** and restructured the app from MVC to **MVVM**, so views react to view-model changes and widgets stop depending on each other. The app became noticeably more stable and much easier to test and extend.

### A schema that bends to the person

The first MongoDB schema centred on a single User document linked to medications, seizure events and triggers. A one-size-fits-all list of triggers turned out not to fit anyone: our very first pilot user reported triggers related to fibromyalgia that no standard list included. So we introduced **user-defined types**: a shared set of common triggers (alcohol, sunlight, heat) that each person can extend with their own.

### TypeScript on the server

I used the rewrite as the moment to move the server from JavaScript to **TypeScript**, with **Typegoose** for the MongoDB models. Strict types caught whole categories of mistakes before they reached testers and made the codebase far easier to navigate.

### Onboarding and health data

A new multi-step onboarding builds a profile of each person's condition. IDEA then connects to **Apple HealthKit** and **Android Health Connect**, so heart rate and other wearable data can sit alongside the journal.

## Phase IV: Coach, Detective and a team (2024)

I came back in 2024 to lead the app's next version, this time with a team. I led and mentored three developers while continuing to write most of the Flutter code. That version added:

- **Coach:** an eScore that summarises how a person is doing, with daily challenges, journal prompts and streaks to keep the habit going.
- **Detective:** a calendar and pattern view that surfaces seizure cycles and time-of-day patterns.
- **Wearable charts:** each logged event shows the heart-rate data from around that moment.
- **Sign in with Apple and Google**, which cut the time it took a new user to get value from the app by roughly 40%.
- **CI/CD on AWS CodePipeline**, which cut deployment time by 90% and made rapid user testing practical.
- Health data stored in **Amazon S3** for later analysis, nested MongoDB documents restructured to cut queries by 30%, and a **Retool** admin so the team could update content without a release.

By the end, I had written more than 630 of the project's roughly 820 commits and about 86% of the Flutter code, along with the first version of the TypeScript backend that grew to 44 endpoints.

## What I took from it

IDEA is where I learned that product and engineering aren't separate jobs. The questions in the journal came from interviews I ran; the schema changed because of one user's fibromyalgia; the architecture changed because the design did. It's also where I learned to pick up a new stack quickly and still ship something people with a real condition could rely on.
